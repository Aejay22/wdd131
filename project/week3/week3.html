WEEK 3: FAMILY HEALTH (CLEANLINESS)

3.1 Meaning of Health

According to the World Health Organization (WHO), health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being, and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity.

The three parts of health:
  • Physical health is when the body works well and is free from disease.
  • Mental health is when the mind is sound, calm and able to think clearly.
  • Social health is when a person gets along well with family, friends and the community.

3.2 Maintaining Good Health

  1. Eat a balanced diet with all food classes.
  2. Drink clean, safe water (about 6 to 8 glasses daily).
  3. Exercise regularly.
  4. Get enough sleep (8 to 10 hours for adolescents).
  5. Practice personal hygiene.
  6. Keep the environment clean.
  7. Go for regular medical check-ups and take immunisation (vaccines).
  8. Avoid harmful substances such as alcohol, tobacco and hard drugs.
  9. Rest and relax; avoid too much stress.
  10. Treat illnesses early at a hospital or clinic, not by self-medication.

3.3 Personal Cleanliness (Personal Hygiene)

Personal hygiene means keeping our body clean and taking care of it to prevent disease.

Parts of personal cleanliness:
Parts How to keep it clean
Skin Bathe at least twice daily with soap and clean water; dry well with a clean towel.
Hair Wash regularly, comb daily, do not share combs or brushes.
Teeth and mouth Brush twice daily (morning and before bed), use floss, reduce sugar, visit the dentist twice a year.
Nails Cut short and keep clean; do not bite them.
Hands Wash with soap and running water before eating, after using the toilet, after handling rubbish and after playing.
Eyes Do not rub with dirty hands; do not read in dim light; do not share eye drops or towels.
Ears Clean the outer ear only; never push sharp objects (like pins or matchsticks) inside.
Feet Wash and dry between the toes; wear clean socks and shoes; cut toenails.
Clothes Wash regularly; wear clean clothes; change underwear daily.
Nose Use a clean handkerchief or tissue; cover the nose when sneezing.
Correct hand-washing steps:
  1. Wet hands with clean running water.
  2. Apply soap.
  3. Rub palms together.
  4. Rub the back of each hand and between the fingers.
  5. Rub the thumbs and fingertips.
  6. Scrub for at least 20 seconds.
  7. Rinse with clean water.
  8. Dry with a clean towel or air dry.

3.4 Cleaning the Home

A clean home keeps the family healthy and free from germs, insects and rodents.

Ways of keeping the home clean:
  1. Sweep and mop the floors daily.
  2. Dust furniture and wipe surfaces with a damp cloth.
  3. Wash dishes and cooking utensils immediately after use with soap and clean water.
  4. Keep food covered and store it safely in a clean place or refrigerator.
  5. Clean the kitchen – wipe counters and sink every day.
  6. Clean the toilet and bathroom with disinfectant regularly.
  7. Wash bedsheets, pillowcases and towels often; air the mattresses and beds in the sun.
  8. Open windows to let in fresh air and sunlight.
  9. Dispose of refuse properly in a covered dustbin; empty it daily.
  10. Clear the surroundings – cut the grass, remove stagnant water, and fill up ditches around the house.
  11. Control pests such as flies, cockroaches, mosquitoes and rats.
  12. Keep drinking water covered and clean the water containers.

Cleaning materials: broom, mop, bucket, brush, soap, detergent, disinfectant, dustpan, dustbin, duster, gloves.

3.5 Advantages of Personal Hygiene

  1. Prevents diseases such as cholera, typhoid, diarrhoea, scabies and ringworm.
  2. Prevents body odour.
  3. Gives a good appearance and boosts confidence.
  4. Makes people accept you in society.
  5. Prevents the spread of germs to others.
  6. Keeps the body fit and healthy.
  7. Saves money that would be spent on treatment.
  8. Prevents tooth decay and bad breath.

3.6 Causes of Poor Hygiene

  1. Poverty – inability to afford soap, water and basic items.
  2. Ignorance – lack of knowledge about hygiene.
  3. Lack of clean water supply.
  4. Laziness or carelessness.
  5. Overcrowding in homes and schools.
  6. Poor housing and bad toilet facilities.
  7. Bad habits and culture or traditions that discourage certain hygiene practices.
  8. Lack of proper waste disposal systems.
  9. Illness or disability that makes self-care difficult.

Diseases caused by poor hygiene: cholera, typhoid fever, dysentery, diarrhoea, worm infestation, scabies, ringworm, tooth decay, malaria (from mosquito breeding in dirty surroundings).
