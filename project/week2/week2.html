WEEK 2: HUMAN DEVELOPMENT

2.1 Meaning of Puberty and Adolescence

Puberty is the period in life when a child's body begins to change into an adult's body. It is the stage when the body becomes able to reproduce. It is caused by hormones released in the body.

Adolescence is the stage of life between childhood and adulthood. It is the period of growth and change, physically, emotionally and socially. In general, adolescence covers about 10 to 19 years of age.

Note: Puberty is the physical part of adolescence. Adolescence is the whole period.

Age of puberty:

  • Girls: usually starts between 8 and 13 years.
  • Boys: usually starts between 9 and 14 years.
  • Every person is different. Starting earlier or later than friends is normal.

Hormones are chemical messengers in the body. At puberty:

  • In girls, the ovaries produce oestrogen.
  • In boys, the testes produce testosterone.

2.2 Physical Changes

Changes common to both boys and girls:
  • Rapid increase in height (growth spurt) and weight.
  • Growth of hair under the armpits and in the pubic area.
  • Increased sweating and body odour.
  • Oily skin and pimples (acne).
  • Increased appetite.
Changes in boys:
  • Voice breaks and become deeper
  • Growth of beards and moustache.
  • Shoulders become broader and muscles develop.
  • Hair grows on the chest.
  • Testes and penis enlarge.
  • Wet dreams may occur (a normal release of semen during sleep).
Changes in girls:
  • Breasts develop.
  • Hips become wider.
  • Menstruation begins (menarche).
  • Body becomes more rounded as fat gathers around the hips and thighs.
  • Voice becomes slightly softer and fuller.

2.3 Emotional Changes

Because of hormones, adolescents may experience:

  • Mood swings (happy one minute, sad or angry the next).
  • Feeling shy or self-conscious about the changing body.
  • Strong feelings of attraction to other people.
  • A desire for privacy and independence.
  • Feeling confused, anxious or stressed.
  • Wanting to be accepted by friends.

How to cope: Talk to a parent, guardian, teacher or counsellor; get enough sleep; exercise; and remember that these feelings are normal and will settle with time.

2.4 Social Changes

  • Spending more time with friends and being strongly influenced by peers (peer pressure).
  • Wanting more freedom and to make personal decisions.
  • Developing interest in the opposite sex.
  • Taking on more responsibilities at home and in school.
  • Developing new interests, talents and goals.
  • Challenges such as peer pressure to take drugs, steal, join bad groups or become sexually active too early.

Advice: Choose good friends, learn to say "NO" to bad behaviour, and obey the guidance of parents and teachers.

2.5 Personal Hygiene During Puberty

Because of increased sweating and oil production, adolescents must practice good hygiene:

  1. Bathe at least twice daily with soap and clean water.
  2. Wash the armpits and private parts thoroughly.
  3. Use deodorant or antiperspirant if needed.
  4. Wear clean underwear daily and change it regularly.
  5. Wash the face twice a day to prevent pimples; do not squeeze pimples.
  6. Brush the teeth twice daily.
  7. Keep hair clean and neat.
  8. Wear clean clothes.
  9. Boys should shave carefully using a clean razor and should not share razors.

2.6 Menstruation and Menstrual Hygiene

Menstruation (a period) is the monthly discharge of blood and tissue from the womb (uterus) through the vagina when a girl or woman is not pregnant.

Menarche is the first menstruation in a girl's life (usually between 9 and 15 years).

The menstrual cycle:
  • The cycle averages 28 days, but may range from 21 to 35 days, especially in the early years.
  • Bleeding lasts about 3 to 7 days.
  • About the middle of the cycle (around day 14), an ovary releases an egg. This is called ovulation.
  • If the egg is not fertilised, the thick lining of the womb breaks down and leaves the body as blood. This is the period.

Signs that a period is about to start: stomach cramps, back pain, breast tenderness, mood changes, tiredness.

Menstrual hygiene (how to stay clean and healthy):
  1. Use clean sanitary pads, tampons or menstrual cups.
  2. Change pads every 4 to 6 hours, even if the flow is light.
  3. Wash the private parts with clean water and mild soap (washing outside only) at least twice daily.
  4. Always wipe or wash from front to back.
  5. Wash hands before and after changing a pad.
  6. Wrap used pads in paper or a small bag and dispose of them in a dustbin. Never flush pads down the toilet.
  7. Bathe daily and wear clean underwear.
  8. Keep a record of period dates on a calendar.
  9. If using cloth, wash it with soap and clean water and dry it in the sun.
  10. Eat healthy food and drink enough water.

Relieving cramps: warm compress on the tummy, rest, light exercise, plenty of water. See a doctor or nurse if the pain is severe.
